# User Guide

The Rugby Junction Train Tracker is a modern day replacement for the now defunct ATCS Monitor application. While the Train Tracker doesn't work exactly the same as ATCS Monitor did, we think you'll find it just as useful once you understand how to use it.

## Getting Started

### Logging In

:::info Access by Invite Only!

The Rugby Junction Train Tracker is _not_ a public web site. You must first be granted access by an administrator before attempting to log in.
:::

Upon visiting [https://rugbyjunction.us](https://rugbyjunction.us), you'll be asked for the email address you provided to an administrator. After clicking the "Send Code" button, check your email for the six digit code and enter it on the next screen, then click "Verify Code".

*   The code is valid for 10 minutes. If it expires, or the email never arrives, click "Resend code".
*   If you do not receive the email, check your "junk" folder and make sure your inbox isn't full (a common problem with Gmail accounts).
*   Check the "Remember me (1 year)" checkbox to stay logged in on that device for a year. Otherwise, you'll be asked to log in again after 12 hours.

### Logging Out

Logging out ends your session and returns you to the login page. To log out, select the hamburger menu on the upper right, click "Log Out", and confirm.

### Supported Web Browsers

The Train Tracker has been tested using Chromium-based web browsers, such as:

*   [Google Chrome](https://www.google.com/chrome/)
*   [Brave](https://brave.com/download/)
*   [Microsoft Edge](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/edge/)

:::warning Apple's Safari browser is not supported

The Rugby Junction Train Tracker does not work with the Apple Safari browser. Please install one of the browsers listed above instead.

:::

## The Map

*   The map remembers where you left it, so it opens at the same location and zoom level on your next visit.
*   If you allow the browser to use your location, your position is shown on the map. Click the target button below the zoom buttons on the upper left to center the map on your location.
*   While the map is open, the Train Tracker keeps your device's screen from going to sleep, so you can watch it trackside without touching your phone.

## Antennas

An antenna is a location along a rail line that is "listening" for a train's telemetry signals, much like rail fans have been doing for years with scanner radios. The railroad the antenna is listening to, such as the Canadian National (CN) or Wisconsin & Southern (WSOR), is displayed on the lower-right corner of the antenna's name tag.

The antenna shown below, Rugby Junction, monitors two separate railroads: the Canadian National and the Wisconsin & Southern. Each railroad gets its own name tag.

<img src={require('@site/static/img/userguide/antenna_multiple_railroads.png').default} alt="Antenna with Multiple Railroads" width="900" />

Some antennas sit at a junction where one railroad's subdivisions meet, such as Junction City. Those subdivisions share a single name tag, and the antenna's history lists each subdivision and its milepost.

### Last Train

Below each name tag, "Last Train" shows the time a train was last detected at the antenna and an arrow pointing in the direction it was heading.

### Online Status

*   An online antenna displays a solid blue dot with a pulsing blue glow. It should be providing nearby train statuses.
*   The blue dotted circle represents a radius of about 2 miles around the antenna, which is the approximate range in which the antenna can detect a train. Depending on many factors, such as the weather, the location of the antenna, terrain, and physical obstructions, the range could be greater or less than 2 miles.

<img src={require('@site/static/img/userguide/antenna_online.png').default} alt="Online Antenna Example" width="450" />

### No Recent Trains

An antenna with a hollow dot inside a solid blue ring is online but hasn't detected a train in a while (6 hours by default). This can simply mean the line is quiet, or it can mean the antenna isn't hearing trains as it should.

<img src={require('@site/static/img/userguide/antenna_no_recent_trains.png').default} alt="No Recent Trains Antenna Example" width="450" />

### Offline Status

*   An offline antenna displays a gray dot and a gray dotted circle. It is not capable of providing train statuses.
*   The subdivision custodian responsible for the antenna is likely aware the antenna is offline and working to get it online as soon as possible.
*   An orange "i" badge on the dot means the custodian left a note about the outage. Click the badge to read it.

<img src={require('@site/static/img/userguide/antenna_offline.png').default} alt="Offline Antenna Example" width="450" />

## Map Pins

A map pin appears at an antenna location when the antenna has detected the presence of a train. Clicking the map pin opens a fly-out with:

*   The antenna name, railroad, subdivision, and milepost.
*   The train's direction and whether it is moving, when known.
*   The time the train was last detected.
*   A train label, such as "Train 7KQ2ZD", or the name you gave the train if you are tracking it. Click the label to track the train (see [Track a Train](#track-a-train)).
*   The types of devices detected on the train: head-of-train (HOT), end-of-train (EOT), and distributed power (DPU).
*   A "Share" link (see [Share a Train](#share-a-train)).

Each new map pin appears at full brightness and gradually fades. The pin disappears 5 minutes after the last signal from the train unless another signal is received to bring it back to full brightness. A pin whose latest signal came from the end-of-train device, or whose only devices are distributed power units, disappears after about 2½ minutes.

<img src={require('@site/static/img/userguide/antenna_flyout.png').default} alt="Antenna Details Fly-Out" width="450" />

:::info Map Pin Location vs Train Location

The location of the map pin, which is usually centered above the antenna location, is not usually the exact location of the train. In most cases, the train is within a 2 mile radius of the antenna. This could mean the locomotive is 2 miles away and approaching the antenna, or the train has already passed the antenna and the end-of-train is already 2 miles past it.

:::

### Map Pin History

To see when the last 10 trains passed an antenna location, click the antenna's name tag or its "Last Train" line to open the map pin history. Each row shows:

*   The time the train was last detected. In other words, the records in the map pin history display the time the train was leaving the antenna location and not when it first arrived.
*   The train's direction and subdivision.
*   The train label and the devices detected on the train.
*   A share button that shares a link to the train (see [Share a Train](#share-a-train)).

Subdivision custodians also see a "Local" switch on each row to mark a train as a local.

<img src={require('@site/static/img/userguide/mappinhistory.png').default} alt="Map Pin History" width="450" />

### Passenger Trains

When passenger train data is available, Amtrak trains appear as round icons with a light blue ring, turned to face the direction of travel. Click one to see the train number, route, heading, time of the last update, and speed.

## Features

The hamburger menu on the upper right holds the settings below, along with "User Guide" and "Log Out". Settings are saved in your browser, so each device keeps its own.

### 12 or 24 Hour Time Setting

The Train Tracker displays times in 24 hour format by default. The menu item shows the current format, "24-hour Format" or "12-hour Format". Click it to switch.

### Light or Dark Mode

The Train Tracker defaults to "dark mode", which displays a dark map and dark backgrounds. The menu item shows the current mode, "Dark Mode" or "Light Mode". Click it to switch.

:::tip Dark Mode Can Save Battery

If you're using the Train Tracker on a mobile device with an OLED screen, dark mode uses less battery power than light mode.

:::

### Mileposts

Milepost numbers are displayed along the tracks when you zoom in closely. Click "Mileposts On" / "Mileposts Off" in the menu to show or hide them.

### Defect Detectors

Defect detectors are shown as orange triangles marked with a "D". Click one to see its location, railroad, division, subdivision, milepost, and radio frequency. Click "Defect Detectors On" / "Defect Detectors Off" in the menu to show or hide them.

### Highlight Railroads

The tracks of selected railroads are drawn brighter and thicker than the rest. By default, the railroads the Train Tracker covers (CN, CPKC, and WSOR) are selected. To change the selection, click "Railroads" in the menu and check the railroads you want highlighted. "All", "None", and "Default" change the selection in one click.

### Track a Train

Perhaps the best feature in the Train Tracker is the ability to track a train as it moves across the railroad's network. This allows you to find a train that's hundreds of miles from your location and know when the train is at an antenna close to you without having to constantly look at the Train Tracker.

There are two places where you can start tracking a train:

*   From the map pin fly-out, click the train label (for example, "Train 7KQ2ZD").

<img src={require('@site/static/img/userguide/tracking_flyout_untracked.png').default} alt="Antenna Details Fly-Out" width="450" />

*   From the map pin history, click the train in the row that has a timestamp close to the train you'd like to track.

<img src={require('@site/static/img/userguide/tracking_mappinhistory.png').default} alt="Tracking via Map Pin History" width="450" />

When the pop-up appears, give the train a symbol of up to 10 characters that is meaningful to you, and then click "Save". When you start from the map pin history, the pop-up is titled "Track This Train" and the button is labeled "Track".

<img src={require('@site/static/img/userguide/tracking_track.png').default} alt="Track a Train" width="450" />

Once tracked, you'll see a colored "T" icon on the map pin _if the train is actually at that map pin_. Once the train is out of range of an antenna, you'll see the symbol you gave the train below the antenna name. The symbol stays at the last antenna the train was detected at and only moves once the train approaches another antenna location.

<img src={require('@site/static/img/userguide/tracking_flyout_tracked.png').default} alt="Antenna Details Fly-Out" width="450" />

To change the symbol or stop tracking the train, click the train's symbol on the map pin fly-out, in the map pin history, or below the antenna name. In the "Enter a Symbol" pop-up, click "Save" to keep a new symbol or "Untrack" to stop tracking.

<img src={require('@site/static/img/userguide/tracking_untrack.png').default} alt="Untrack a Train" width="450" />

Tracking ends automatically 12 hours after it starts.

Your tracked trains follow you across devices. If you start tracking on one device, such as a laptop computer, then go trackside with just your phone, your tracked trains will still be visible on your phone and vice versa!

### Share a Train

To send a train to another Train Tracker user, click "Share" on the map pin fly-out or the share button in the map pin history. If your browser supports it, your device's share options open. Otherwise, the link is copied so you can paste it into a message.

When the other user opens the link, the train is added to their tracked trains, using the symbol you gave it if you are tracking it. Shared links only work while the train is still active on the map, and the other user must have their own Train Tracker login.
